## App Summaries

### Freedom
Freedom is a cross-platform distraction blocker used by over 3 million people.
It blocks websites and apps across Mac, Windows, iOS, and Android simultaneously.
Key features include scheduled blocking sessions, Locked Mode (cannot be
disabled mid-session), custom blocklists, and focus sounds. Priced at
$39.99/year. Its strength is cross-device sync, one session blocks
distractions on all your devices at once.

### Cold Turkey
Cold Turkey is the most aggressive distraction blocker available, operating
at the OS level rather than just inside a browser. Its most extreme feature,
"Frozen Turkey," locks you out of your entire computer until the timer expires
no override, no escape. It is desktop-only (no mobile app) and uses a
one-time purchase model ($39 for one device) rather than a subscription.
Best suited for people who need hard enforcement and have failed with softer tools.

### Tiimo
Tiimo is a visual daily planner built specifically for ADHD and Autism users.
Instead of text-based to-do lists, it displays your day as a colour-coded
visual timeline so you can literally see time passing. It includes AI task
breakdown, mood tracking, widgets, and gentle transition reminders rather
than jarring alarms. Won iPhone App of the Year at the 2025 App Store Awards.
Used by over 500,000 people. Priced around $54.99/year.

### Routinery
Routinery is a step-by-step routine execution app used by 5 million people.
Rather than just listing tasks, it walks you through your routine one step at
a time using a timer, you always know exactly what to do next. It recently
added voice-guided timers that announce each task aloud, which is a major
feature for users with time blindness or ADHD. Named Best ADHD App by Forbes
Health 2025. Priced at $39.99/year.

## Reflection

### What makes Focus Bear different?
Most competitors focus on one problem. Freedom and Cold Turkey block
distractions but don't help you build routines or habits. Tiimo helps
with visual planning but doesn't block distractions. Routinery guides you
through routines but doesn't block apps or track habits long-term. Focus Bear
is the only app in this group that attempts to address the full picture of
executive dysfunction — helping users start their day, stay focused, avoid
distractions, take breaks, and build habits — all in one place, with
neurodivergent users at the centre of the design.

### If I were a user, why would I choose Focus Bear?
If I had ADHD and struggled with all aspects of my day — not just one —
Focus Bear would make the most sense. I wouldn't want to pay for three
separate apps (a blocker, a routine app, and a habit tracker) and context-
switch between them. Focus Bear removes that friction by doing it all in one
place. The movement break reminders are also something no other app in this
list offers, which matters for physical and mental health during long work days.

### One feature a competitor has that Focus Bear doesn't
**Tiimo's visual timeline** is something Focus Bear currently lacks. For users
with time blindness — a very common ADHD experience — being able to see time
visually draining away is more useful than a text-based schedule. Tiimo's
colour-coded timeline with real-time progress makes time feel tangible in a
way that lists simply cannot. This is a meaningful gap for the exact audience
Focus Bear is trying to serve.

### One improvement I think Focus Bear could make
Based on this research, I think Focus Bear could benefit from adding a
**visual timeline or calendar view** similar to Tiimo — showing the user's
planned routines and focus sessions as visual blocks across the day. This
would help users with time blindness see their day at a glance and understand
how much time is left before transitions, rather than relying on text
reminders alone. It would also make Focus Bear feel more complete as a
full-day planning tool rather than a series of separate features.

---

## Honest Assessment
Focus Bear is not always the best tool for every user. If someone only needs
distraction blocking, Cold Turkey or Freedom are more powerful and cheaper.
If someone needs beautiful visual planning, Tiimo is more refined. If someone
just needs routine guidance, Routinery has more users and polish. Focus Bear's
value is in the combination — and that only matters if the user actually needs
all of those things together. For ADHD users who struggle across the board,
it makes a strong case. For users with one specific problem, a specialist app
may serve them better.
