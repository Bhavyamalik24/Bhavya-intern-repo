# 🔒 Cyber Security Guidelines - Reflection

## 🔍 Research Summary

### Common cyber security threats in remote work
- **Phishing emails** — fake emails pretending to be legitimate services to 
  steal credentials
- **Weak or reused passwords** — one breach exposes multiple accounts
- **Unsecured Wi-Fi** — public networks can be intercepted by attackers
- **Unattended unlocked screens** — anyone nearby can access your data
- **Malware and ransomware** — malicious software that steals or locks your data
- **Social engineering** — manipulating people into revealing sensitive info
- **Outdated software** — unpatched vulnerabilities that attackers exploit

### Best practices for keeping devices and accounts secure
- Use strong, unique passwords for every account
- Enable 2FA on all work accounts
- Keep software and operating systems up to date
- Only use trusted, secure Wi-Fi networks for work
- Use a VPN when working from public locations
- Never install unauthorised software on work devices
- Lock your screen whenever stepping away, even briefly

### Why lock your computer when away?
An unlocked screen is an open door — anyone nearby can access files, emails, 
accounts, and sensitive data in seconds. This applies even at home, as 
visitors or family members could accidentally or intentionally access 
confidential information.

### How to handle phishing attempts
- Do not click links in unsolicited emails — go directly to the website instead
- Check the sender's actual email address, not just the display name
- Look for urgency, spelling errors, or requests for credentials — red flags
- When in doubt, do not click — report it to your supervisor
- Never enter passwords after clicking a link in an email

### What makes a strong password?
- At least 12–16 characters long
- Mix of uppercase, lowercase, numbers, and symbols
- Not based on personal info (name, birthday, etc.)
- Unique — never reused across accounts
- Best practice: use a password manager to generate and store them securely

### Why is 2FA important?
Even if a password is stolen, 2FA means an attacker still cannot access your 
account without the second factor (usually your phone). It is the single most 
effective way to protect accounts from unauthorised access.

## 📝 Reflection

### What security measures do I currently follow?
- I use different passwords for most accounts
- I have 2FA enabled on some personal accounts

### Where can I improve?
- Set up a proper password manager for all accounts
- Enable 2FA on GitHub and all Focus Bear work tools
- Make screen locking an automatic habit (Win + L every time I step away)
- Be more cautious about links in emails and messages

### How will I make secure behaviour a habit?
I will treat security steps the same way I treat saving a file — automatic and 
non-negotiable. Locking my screen, checking email senders, and using the 
password manager will become part of my daily routine rather than something 
I think about occasionally.

### What steps will I take to secure my passwords and accounts?
- Set up a password manager (e.g. Bitwarden — free and open source)
- Update any weak or reused passwords for work accounts
- Enable 2FA on GitHub, Discord, and any other Focus Bear tools I am given access to
- Store recovery codes for 2FA accounts securely

### What would I do if I suspected a security breach?
1. Do not panic or try to fix it alone
2. Immediately change the password for the affected account
3. Report it to my supervisor straight away
4. Document what happened — what I noticed, when, and what accounts may be affected
5. Follow Focus Bear's Security Breach Management process
