# Getting to Know the Focus Bear Product

## Key Things I Learned Relevant to Data Analytics

### 1. Two Distinct Break Types Generate Different Data
Focus Bear has two break types — Pomodoro breaks (5 minutes, full app
access) and Micro breaks (shorter, guided movement or eye rest). From
a data analytics perspective, these generate separate engagement metrics.
Tracking which break type users prefer and whether break frequency
correlates with longer focus sessions would be a meaningful analysis.

### 2. Cuddly Bear vs Grizzly Bear Mode
Focus Bear has two blocking intensity modes — Cuddly Bear (softer,
more flexible blocking) and Grizzly Bear (strict, harder to bypass).
Understanding which mode users choose and whether mode selection
correlates with habit completion rates or focus session length would
be valuable product insight.

### 3. Work-to-Break Ratio Feature
The app tracks the ratio of work time to break time and helps users
optimise their cycles. This is a directly analytics-relevant feature —
the data generated (work duration, break duration, ratio over time)
is exactly the kind of time-series data that would show whether users
are improving their focus discipline over time.

### 4. Meeting Mode with Automated Detection
Focus Bear automatically detects when a user is in a meeting and
adjusts blocking behaviour accordingly. This generates event data
(meeting detected, blocking paused, meeting ended) that could be
analysed to understand how meetings impact focus session patterns
throughout the day.

### 5. Office Mode vs WFH Mode
Users can switch between Office Mode and Work From Home Mode, which
likely affects which apps and sites are blocked. This segmentation
is analytically useful — comparing engagement metrics between office
and WFH sessions could reveal whether the working environment affects
habit completion and focus duration.

## Reflection

### What did I learn about the product that I didn't know before?
I wasn't aware of the depth of Focus Bear's blocking customisation.
The ability to allow specific subreddits while blocking the rest of
Reddit, the distinction between always-blocked sites and focus-session-
blocked sites, and the two blocking intensity modes (Cuddly vs Grizzly)
all show that the app is designed for nuanced, personalised use rather
than a blunt all-or-nothing approach. This is particularly relevant to
neurodivergent users who need flexibility alongside structure.

I also didn't know about the Work-to-Break Ratio feature — this is a
sophisticated productivity metric that goes beyond simple habit tracking
and directly measures the balance of a user's work patterns over time.

### Did I spot anything in the help centre that seems out of date?

Yes — a few potential inconsistencies worth flagging:

1. **Advanced Settings article says "not yet available on Windows"** —
   during my first-day testing of the app on Windows, some advanced
   settings were accessible. This article may be outdated if Windows
   support has since been added.

2. **iOS install via TestFlight** — the help article for iOS installation
   references TestFlight, which is typically used for beta/internal builds.
   If Focus Bear is now publicly available on the App Store, this article
   may need updating to reflect the standard installation path.

3. **Two duplicate articles on Super Pomodoro Mode** — there are two
   separate help articles covering Super Pomodoro Mode with very similar
   content ("Super Pomodoro Mode in Focus Bear" and "Unleashing Focus
   Power with Super Pomodoro Mode"). These could be consolidated into
   one article to avoid confusion.

4. **Completed habits disappearing after morning routine** — based on
   my first-day user testing, after completing morning habits the app
   shows a "you're all done" message and the habit list disappears.
   There is no help article explaining this behaviour or how to review
   completed habits, which could confuse new users who want to see what
   they accomplished.
