# 📋 Agile Workflows & Kanban - Reflection

## 🔍 Research Summary

### How does a Kanban board work?
A Kanban board is a visual workflow management tool that shows the status of 
tasks at a glance. Tasks are represented as cards that move from left to right 
across columns as work progresses. It helps teams see what is being worked on, 
what is blocked, and what is done, all in one place.

### What do the columns represent?
- **Backlog / Not Started:** Tasks that exist but haven't been picked up yet
- **In Progress:** Tasks actively being worked on right now
- **Blocked:** Tasks that cannot move forward due to a dependency or issue
- **Ready for Review:** Tasks completed and waiting for feedback or approval
- **Approved / Done:** Tasks that have been reviewed and closed

### How do tasks move through the board?
Tasks are created in the Backlog and assigned to a team member. That person 
moves the card to "In Progress" when they start, "Ready for Review" when done, 
and it moves to "Done" once approved. The person working on the task is 
responsible for keeping their cards updated, not the manager.

### Benefits of limiting Work in Progress (WIP)
- Forces focus on finishing tasks before starting new ones
- Reduces context switching which kills productivity
- Makes bottlenecks visible, if one column fills up, the team can see the 
  problem and fix it
- Improves quality because you are not juggling too many things at once

## 📝 Reflection

### How does Kanban help manage priorities and avoid overload?
Kanban makes it impossible to hide how much work is actually in flight. When 
everything is visible on the board, it becomes clear when someone is overloaded 
or when a task has been sitting in one column too long. WIP limits force you to 
finish what you started rather than constantly picking up new tasks, which 
prevents the feeling of being busy but getting nothing done.

### How can I improve my workflow using Kanban principles?
During my internship I will use the GitHub Projects board to track my onboarding 
issues. I will only move one or two issues to "In Progress" at a time, complete 
them properly, and move them through to "Done" before picking up the next one. 
This will help me stay focused rather than jumping between tasks.
